After a long break caused by positive real-life events, I can finally continue this project.

During that time, I had plenty of opportunities to rethink the overall direction and decided to make some significant changes to the development approach.

## What will change?

Instead of trying to build the final rover design right away, I will switch to a heavily iterative development process. The focus will be on quickly testing and refining ideas using placeholder parts and simplified implementations before committing to the final design.

This approach will result in a rougher-looking prototype at first, but it better supports the exploratory nature of this project. Rapid iteration is more valuable at this stage than visual polish.

To achieve this, many complex components will be simplified and broken down into smaller subassemblies. While this may reduce the structural strength of the prototype, it will make wiring, electronics integration, and overall chassis development much easier. The obvious downside is that many parts will need to be designed twice—once for experimentation and once for the final implementation.

---

Another addition to the project will be a dedicated test stand for the rover. This will provide a much safer environment for developing and testing motors, electronics, and software before the rover operates on the ground.

I am also more open to incorporating metal components where additional rigidity or stability is beneficial.

Although it is not the primary goal, I will aim for the final rover to be capable of operating outdoors in dry conditions. At its intended size, it will simply be too large for anything beyond basic driving tests indoors.

Finally, I plan to include extensive visual feedback on both the rover and the test stand. Small displays, status LEDs, and other indicators should make it possible to understand the rover's current state without constantly relying on a separate control screen.

And, admittedly, it will look pretty cool.
